\title{STRATA: Staged Evaluation of Temporal--Statistical Claims over Annotated Clinical Timelines}

\begin{abstract}
Language-model systems increasingly claim to test hypotheses against data,
but existing benchmarks score only the final answer and hand the system the
relevant records already assembled. Over a corpus of annotated clinical
timelines, a claim such as ``after the Pfizer vaccine, headache tends to
precede fatigue'' first requires deciding \emph{which reports count}, what the
null is and which exact test applies; each decision can be wrong while the
verdict is right. Statistical-reasoning benchmarks and hypothesis-testing
agents score none of these steps. We present \textsc{Strata}, a staged
evaluation framework and benchmark for temporal-statistical claims. Claims
are generated mechanically from the annotated timelines by a template-driven
process that enumerates the cores that exist in the corpus, computes every
stage of the gold (the plan, the exact evidence sets, the exact $p$-values
and a three-decision verdict with sub-typed abstention) and validates it by
mechanical invariants and a deterministic oracle, so that the corpus
codebook, the stipulations the labels depend on, is part of the task rather
than hidden in the labels. The
framework orchestrates verification as plan, retrieve, test, each stage with
an output contract, a bounded verify-and-repair loop and a gold counterpart,
so a system is scored on its plan, its evidence and its abstention, not the
verdict alone, and any stage can be filled with gold to measure the loss of
the others. Across five models from a 2024 open 32B pair to current frontier
models, the codebook is what frontier models need, and the plan
contract is what lets an open model match them with auditable evidence. The
denominator is where systems fail, and the verdict hides it.
\end{abstract}
