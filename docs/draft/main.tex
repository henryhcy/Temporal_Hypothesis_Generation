\title{STRATA: Multistage, Evidence-Grounded Statistical Verification of Temporal Hypotheses in Clinical Timelines}
%% working title

  \city{}
  \country{}

\begin{abstract}
Language-model systems increasingly claim to test hypotheses against data,
yet benchmarks still score only the final answer and hand the system the
relevant records already assembled. We introduce temporal hypothesis
verification, a task in which the decisions before any test is run are
themselves evaluated: for a claim such as ``after Moderna, Pain typically
occurs before Asthenia'', a system must decide \emph{which reports count},
what the null is and which exact test applies, and each decision can be
wrong while the verdict is right. \textsc{Strata} is, to our knowledge, the
first benchmark with gold for every such decision: the plan, the exact
evidence sets, the exact $p$-values and a three-decision verdict with
sub-typed abstention, generated mechanically from 3{,}166 clinical
timelines and reproduced by a deterministic oracle. The corpus codebook, the
rules the labels depend on, ships as text that any system can be given. We
also introduce \textsc{Art} (articulate, retrieve, test), a staged
verification paradigm in which every decision is an explicit output with a
contract, a bounded verify-and-repair loop and a gold counterpart, so any
stage can be scored or replaced by gold. Across five models, from a 2024
open 32B pair to current frontier models, frontier models verify the claims
once they are given the codebook, and the plan contract carries a 31B open
model to within 0.02 of them with auditable evidence. The evaluation exposes
a verdict--evidence gap: systems fail mostly at the temporal conditioning of
the denominator, and verdict accuracy does not reveal it.
\end{abstract}
