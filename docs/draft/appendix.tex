identifiers it placed in each arm, so every wrongly included or dropped report

queries; what remains for the Straight-to-SQL model is semantic --- co-existing

\paragraph{The codebook as information, one change at a time.} Three
prompt-level decisions were each made as a single controlled change on the
development subset, with everything else fixed. \emph{One missing
stipulation:} under an earlier codebook the 32B planner composed every
association claim (C-V2) as one-arm co-occurrence --- 0 of 9 records ---
although the ``compare the rate with and without $A$'' situation was stated;
the three association wordings were never bridged to it, while ``occur
together'' matched the co-occurrence stipulation. Adding that bridge moved the
planner from 0.900 to 0.960, with 23 of 26 groups unchanged record for record
(C-V2 0.00 $\rightarrow$ 0.67; P-V2 0.33 $\rightarrow$ 0.50; F-R3 0.90
$\rightarrow$ 1.00). A model is not wrong about a situation it was never told exists.
\emph{Schema presentation:} showing the SQL model the \texttt{CREATE VIEW}
bodies of the helper views led it to copy the views' internal aliases into its
own queries (97 rejected statements of 227 in cell A); listing the views as
columns with their semantics in comments removed that error but left an
ambiguous unqualified identifier in self-joins (107 rejected); one sentence in
the row contract removed that too (35 rejected). Cell A went 0.330
$\rightarrow$ 0.415 $\rightarrow$ 0.505 and the SQL stage with the gold plan
0.810 $\rightarrow$ 0.830 $\rightarrow$ 0.900. None of these changed the data,
the models or the method; they are the cost of a prompt artefact that would
otherwise have read as a finding about the new claim families.
