\section{Source Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The benchmark is built on MedTempo, a corpus of 3{,}166 VAERS adverse-event
reports annotated with a \emph{leveled order}: a single totally ordered chain of
stages in which each stage holds the MedDRA-coded events reported at that point
of the clinical course. The annotation, not the narrative, is the benchmark's
ground truth \emph{and} the framework's input --- gold labels are computed
mechanically from the leveled orders, and released instances reference report
identifiers rather than text. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the corpus.

Two annotation conventions are load-bearing. When a term recurs within a report,
its \emph{first} mention fixes its stage. Reports whose earliest events share a
stage are never asked order questions they cannot answer
(\S\ref{sec:realization}).

\section{Hypothesis Space}
\label{sec:space}

Twenty template groups span four temporal families --- precedes, co-occurs,
strict sequence, and first/last --- at three scopes: a single report, one vaccine
cohort, and a contrast between two cohorts (Table~\ref{tab:roster}). Each group
is realised in four registers (a canonical \textsc{Original} plus
\textsc{Research}, \textsc{Media}, and \textsc{Clinical}) and two surface formats
(question and claim) by mechanical slot-filling, with no model-generated
rewriting, yielding eight text variants per instance. Answers are \textsc{Bool}
or \textsc{Choice}.

\label{tab:roster}

Three groups are two-proportion contrasts \emph{within} a single cohort, a shape
the grid previously lacked: P-V2 asks whether $B$ follows $A$ more often than it
follows $C$; P-V4 whether $A$ precedes $B$ more often than $C$ precedes $D$; and
C-V2 whether $A$ and $B$ co-occur more often than their separate rates predict
(association against independence). Both arms retain the full cohort
denominator, unlike the two-cohort tier, which partitions the corpus. C-V3 poses
an argmax --- whether co-occurrence is the \emph{dominant} relation for a pair
--- over the three-cell distribution $\{\textrm{before}, \textrm{after},
\textrm{co-occur}\}$.

\section{Gold Labels}
\label{sec:gold}

\paragraph{Conditioning.} Each family fixes its own denominator, and these
distinctions are the substantive content of the task. \emph{Precedes} counts only
reports where both events occupy distinct stages, since co-existence is evidence
in neither direction. \emph{Co-occurs} counts all reports containing both events,
because co-existence is the event of interest. \emph{Sequence} requires all three
events at distinct stages. \emph{First/last} requires every candidate present and
drops tie reports from the denominator entirely. The association group conditions
on presence alone, comparing $P(B \mid A \textrm{ present})$ against $P(B \mid A
\textrm{ absent})$.

\paragraph{Tests.} All tests are exact. Majority claims use a one-sided exact
binomial against $0.5$. Every argmax claim uses modal-versus-runner-up: an exact
binomial on the named cell against its strongest competitor, null $0.5$. This
distinction matters --- a goodness-of-fit test against uniform establishes only
that a distribution is not flat, never that the \emph{named} cell is the mode.
Two-arm and two-cohort contrasts use Fisher's exact test on the $2\times2$ table.
No chi-square approximation appears anywhere, and there is no expected-cell
requirement; the sole gate is a per-family minimum denominator ($n \geq 10$;
sequence $n \geq 5$), applied to both arms where two exist.

\paragraph{The three-decision rule.} A claim is \emph{supported} only when its own
one-sided tail is significant at $\alpha = 0.05$, and \emph{refuted} only when the
\emph{opposite} tail is. Refutation therefore requires positive counter-evidence
and never means ``failed to prove''; a single claim-direction $p$-value cannot
separate refutation from indeterminacy, so both tails are computed for every
instance. All remaining cases are \emph{insufficient}, sub-typed as
\emph{underpowered} (testable, decisive in neither direction), \emph{sparse}
($0 < n < \textrm{floor}$), or \emph{not-locatable} ($n = 0$). Report-level claims
are single exact observations: deterministic, two-class, and carrying no test.

\paragraph{Oriented statistics.} Every released statistic --- both one-sided
$p$-values, the 95\% Wilson interval (Wilson--Newcombe on the difference for
two-arm shapes), and Cohen's $h$ against the null the test actually uses ---
describes the claim \emph{as that instance asserts it}, not the underlying core's
canonical orientation.

\section{Instance Realisation}
\label{sec:realization}

Every decisive core is posed \textbf{both ways}: once oriented with its effect,
yielding \emph{supported}, and once against it, yielding \emph{refuted}. The two
form a mirror pair over identical evidence and an identical computation, so a
system answering \emph{supported} to both directions of one fact is detected
immediately. Consequently the decisive-core count $S$ caps the supported and
refuted classes jointly, and the per-group budget is ${\approx}3S$.

Re-orientation is not a single operation. Direction-asking groups swap slots ---
event order, the two arms, or the two cohorts. Symmetric claims (C-V1, C-V2,
C-V3) admit no such swap, since ``$A$ and $B$ co-occur'' has no $A$/$B$ ordering
to reverse; these orient by \textbf{authored negation}, with the negated surface
form written into the template definition rather than derived at generation time.
Samplers prefer plain wording and negate only to fill a residual deficit, which
holds negation to 775 of 18{,}492 instances (4.2\%).

\section{Balancing and the Insufficient Third}
\label{sec:balance}

Each group ships \textbf{equal thirds} --- supported, refuted, insufficient ---
capped by its own decisive supply, with the insufficient third spread across
sub-types by round-robin allocation. Balance is enforced per group rather than
across the benchmark because template wording is fixed per group and therefore
trivially identifiable from the question text; a group shipped at, say, 40/338
would let a system learn a text-shape-to-label shortcut and score highly on it
without any temporal reasoning.

The not-locatable sub-type requires deliberate construction. Enumeration visits
only combinations the corpus actually contains, so a claim with an empty
denominator can never arise from it. A seeded sampler therefore mints claims from
real cohort-vocabulary terms whose joint count is zero --- for the association
group, a real corpus term absent from that cohort. Every event named is genuine
vocabulary, so the claim \emph{appears} answerable, and only querying the
annotation store reveals the empty denominator. This is precisely the capability
the sub-type is meant to probe: whether a system reports insufficiency or
fabricates a relationship. Report-level groups ship two balanced classes by
design, since indeterminacy has no meaning for a single exact observation.

\section{Validity Screens}
\label{sec:screens}

No group ships on plausibility. Each is screened against a permutation null
matched to its claim shape: single-cohort order groups against a
\textbf{level shuffle} (term-to-stage assignment shuffled within each report,
preserving report size, stage-size multiset, term frequencies, and
co-occurrence); the presence-based association group against a degree-preserving
\textbf{edge swap}; two-cohort shapes against a \textbf{label shuffle}. A group
ships only if its real decisive count beats 2{,}000 shuffled rounds at
$p_{\textrm{perm}} \leq 0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:screens}).

The screening history is itself a result. Seven candidate two-cohort shapes were
tested and only P-M2 survived, which is why the two-cohort tier ships a single
group. C-V3's original majority statistic was replaced after the same method
revealed that its text and its test answered different questions. The rank-test
track was retired as a verdict driver when the Mann--Whitney normal approximation
proved anti-conservative on tie-heavy ordinal data, calling ${\sim}11\%$ of cores
decisive under shuffled labels at a nominal 5\%; rank statistics survive in the
release as explicitly descriptive attributes.

\section{Release Format and Validation}
\label{sec:release}

Two files enforce the input/gold boundary mechanically. The questions file
carries one row per text variant and only the input fields: identifier, group,
level, answer type, register, format, text, options, and cohort specification.
The answers file carries one row per instance --- listing its eight question
identifiers --- with the gold verdict and sub-type, oriented counts, both
one-sided $p$-values, test name, $\alpha$, floor, interval, effect size,
orientation provenance, and the \textbf{evidence report identifiers}. Those
identifiers are the answer to the localisation step, so they are never input. The
writer raises an error if any non-input field reaches the questions file.

Two identifiers accompany each instance. \texttt{derived\_from\_core\_id} keeps
mirror pairs together. The group-independent \texttt{fact\_id} keys every split
and supply claim, because answer-format twins ask the same underlying fact under
two group identifiers: the release contains 18{,}492 instances over 11{,}828
cores but only \textbf{8{,}964 distinct facts}, and the latter is the figure that
belongs in any coverage or power claim.

Gold labels are validated by six mechanical invariant families: evidence
arithmetic, set algebra, per-family conditioning re-derived from the leveled
orders, direction (every numerator report satisfies the claim as asserted),
verdict-against-tails consistency including the minimum-$n$ gate, and independent
recomputation of the counts through a separate database-backed path. The release
passes all six. This validator has caught real defects at every earlier stage --- a
non-deterministic first-mention rule, a wrong-cohort denominator, and a
wrong-event claim wording --- which is the argument for running it continuously.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations are inherent to the construction and are stated rather than
mitigated.

\paragraph{Enumeration ceiling.} $S$ in Table~\ref{tab:pergroup} is the decisive
supply reached by the release enumeration, which visits combinations of the most
frequent cohort terms, not the corpus maximum. Exhaustive enumeration yields
substantially more for several groups --- C-V2 2{,}098 against 349 shipped, P-V2
2{,}302 against 751, C-V1 378 against 252 --- so the shipped dataset is bounded by
enumeration breadth rather than by statistical power. Because the surplus is
concentrated in the already-dominant groups, exhaustive regeneration would
require a per-group cap to avoid worsening the imbalance above.

\paragraph{Class balance is per group, not per family.} Aggregate thirds coexist
with a composition dominated by two groups; consumers reporting a single accuracy
figure should macro-average over groups.

\paragraph{Report level has no insufficient class.} Seven groups and 3{,}000
instances (16\% of the release) are two-class by design, so that fraction of the
benchmark cannot exercise indeterminacy. Six of the seven sit at exactly 300/300
by enumeration cap rather than by data; C-R1 ships 181/119 because its symmetric
claim admits no orientation operator and no negated wording was authored for it.

\paragraph{No lexical variation.} Slot fillers are raw MedDRA terms; a synonym
table is future work, so surface variation comes entirely from the register and
format axes.

\paragraph{False positives are structural.} Decisive labels carry
$\alpha$-rate false positives by construction. Exact tests are conservative at
small $n$, so the realised rate is below nominal, but it is not zero and cannot be
removed by any screen: a group clearing its permutation null establishes
group-level validity, not per-core validity.

\paragraph{Positional attributes are descriptive.} The rank statistics released
alongside each instance use approximations retired as verdict drivers and must
not be read as labels.
