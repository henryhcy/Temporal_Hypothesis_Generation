\begin{abstract}
%% DRAFT abstract written from the project's framing (docs/plan, 2026-10-08);
%% every number is a v1.2 release statistic. Rewrite before submission.
We study the verification of temporal--statistical claims --- ``after vaccine
$v$, event $A$ tends to precede event $B$'' --- against expert-annotated
timelines of vaccine adverse-event reports, where a verdict must rest on an
explicit denominator, a stated null and an exact test. We release MedTempo
v1.2, a benchmark of 8,763 claim instances (70,104 renderings across four
registers and two formats) in 29 groups and seven claim families over report,
cohort and two-cohort tiers, in which every stage of the gold computation ---
the plan, the evidence sets and the exact test --- is released alongside the
three-decision verdict (supported / refuted / insufficient), and report
identifiers are pseudonymised so that no public lookup is possible. We
evaluate systems with an instrumented reference decomposition (plan, retrieve,
test) whose stage-level ground truth yields stage metrics beyond verdict
accuracy, including denominator agreement, faithfulness and abstention, and we
place end-to-end, agentic, counts-given and externally adapted baselines on the
stages they replace. Across local and frontier models the dominant failure is
the denominator, which verdict accuracy alone conceals; a bounded
verify-and-repair round per stage recovers part of it.
\end{abstract}
