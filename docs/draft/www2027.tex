\begin{abstract}
Language-model systems increasingly promise to test hypotheses against data,
and benchmarks score them on the final $p$-value or decision with the relevant
data already assembled. For a claim such as ``after the Pfizer vaccine,
headache tends to precede fatigue'' over a corpus of annotated adverse-event
timelines, that final step is the easy one: a system must first decide which
reports count, what ``no effect'' means and which test applies, and a verdict
can be right while every one of those decisions is wrong. We release
\textsc{MedTempo-Hypo}, a benchmark of 8{,}763 temporal--statistical claims
(70{,}104 renderings) in 29 groups and seven claim families over three cohort
tiers, built on expert-annotated timelines of 3{,}166 vaccine adverse-event
reports, in which every stage of the gold computation is released with the
three-decision verdict: the plan (events, denominator rule, null, test), the
exact evidence sets, and the exact $p$-values. Identifiers are pseudonymised and
the corpus conventions are shipped as part of the task. We pair it with an
instrumented reference pipeline --- plan, retrieve, test --- whose stage
contracts turn the verdict into auditable quantities (plan agreement, evidence-set
agreement, faithfulness, abstention) and whose prompt frames make the
\emph{information} a system is told and the \emph{architecture} it is given
separately controllable. Across local and agentic systems the denominator is
where verification fails and the verdict hides it: the strongest end-to-end
text-to-SQL baseline is right 66\% of the time but stands on the correct
evidence for 53\% of its right answers, and an agentic baseline decides 64\%
of the claims the data cannot decide. Decomposition pays only when the plan is
a contract between stages: a 32B pipeline whose plan the harness resolves for
retrieval verifies claims at 0.865 with its evidence right 78\% of the time,
0.20 above a verified end-to-end control; one missing stipulation in the
stated conventions moved the same planner by 0.05 and a whole claim family by
0.67.
\end{abstract}
