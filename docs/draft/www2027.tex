\begin{abstract}
Language-model systems increasingly promise to test hypotheses against data,
and benchmarks score them on the final $p$-value or decision with the relevant
data already assembled. For a claim such as ``after the Pfizer vaccine,
headache tends to precede fatigue'' over a corpus of annotated adverse-event
timelines, that final step is the easy one: a system must first decide which
reports count, what ``no effect'' means and which test applies, and a verdict
can be right while every one of those decisions is wrong. We release
\textsc{MedTempo-Hypo}, a benchmark of temporal--statistical claims in 26
groups and seven claim families over three cohort tiers, built on
expert-annotated timelines of 3{,}166 vaccine adverse-event reports, in which
every stage of the gold computation is released with the three-decision
verdict: the plan (events, denominator rule, null, test), the exact evidence
sets and the exact $p$-values. The corpus conventions are shipped as part of
the task, identifiers are pseudonymised, and the release is a development set
and a fact-disjoint 1{,}500-claim test set. We pair it with an instrumented
reference pipeline --- plan, retrieve, test --- whose stage contracts turn the
verdict into auditable quantities (plan agreement, evidence-set agreement,
faithfulness, abstention) and whose prompt frames make the \emph{information}
a system is told and the \emph{architecture} it is given separately
controllable. On the test set, with five models from a 2024 open 32B pair to
Claude Opus~5.5 and GPT-5.6, the stated conventions are the information the
task measures: frontier models verify 1.000 and 0.984 of claims end to end
with them and 0.88 without. For open models the plan contract is what makes a
verdict stand on the right evidence: a 2024 pair goes from 0.575 end to end to
0.810, a 2026 31B model from 0.859 to 0.981 --- within 0.02 of the frontier at
zero API cost --- and the share of right verdicts resting on the exact
denominator rises from 40--86\% to 86--97\%. A published agentic baseline
reaches 0.605 while deciding most of the claims the data cannot decide. The
denominator is where systems fail, and the verdict hides it.
\end{abstract}
