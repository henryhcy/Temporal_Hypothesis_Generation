\begin{abstract}
Language-model systems increasingly claim to test hypotheses against data,
but existing benchmarks score only the final answer and hand the system the
relevant records already assembled. Over a corpus of annotated clinical
timelines, a claim such as ``after the Pfizer vaccine, headache tends to
precede fatigue'' first requires deciding \emph{which reports count}, what the
null is and which exact test applies; each decision can be wrong while the
verdict is right. Statistical-reasoning benchmarks and hypothesis-testing
agents score none of these steps. We present \textsc{Strata}, a staged
evaluation framework and benchmark for temporal--statistical claims. The
benchmark releases, for every claim, the gold of every stage: the plan, the
exact evidence sets and the exact $p$-values, with a three-decision verdict
and sub-typed abstention; the corpus conventions are shipped as part of the
task. The framework orchestrates verification as plan, retrieve, test, each
stage with an output contract, a bounded verify-and-repair loop and a gold
counterpart, so a system is scored on plan agreement, evidence-set agreement,
faithfulness and abstention, not the verdict alone, and so stages can be
filled with gold to measure each one's loss. On a 1{,}500-claim test set with
five models from a 2024 open 32B pair to Claude Opus~5.5 and GPT-5.6, the
stated conventions are what frontier models need (1.000 and 0.984 with them,
0.88 without), while open models need the plan to be a contract between
stages: a 31B model rises from 0.859 end to end to 0.981, within 0.02 of the
frontier at zero API cost, with the share of right verdicts resting on the
exact evidence rising from 86\% to 94\%. The denominator is where systems
fail, and the verdict hides it.
\end{abstract}
