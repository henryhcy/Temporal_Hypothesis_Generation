\section*{What this benchmark is}
A benchmark for \emph{temporal hypothesis verification} over clinical narratives (VAERS reports in the MedTempo corpus). Each item gives a framework a set of raw narratives plus one natural-language hypothesis about a temporal pattern; the framework must locate the evidence and return \textbf{supported}, \textbf{refuted}, or \textbf{insufficient}. Gold labels are computed deterministically from expert timelines. The design is \textbf{24 template groups}: \textbf{17 hypothesis-test groups} (scored over the whole corpus below) and \textbf{7 report-level groups} (single-narrative facts, deterministic---no test).
\section*{Design principles}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Every cohort-level claim is a hypothesis test with a principled null}---a chance baseline (a coin-flip $0.5$, a uniform distribution, or ``this ordering beats its nearest rival''), or a comparison to another group. No claim is graded by matching an observed number.
\item \textbf{Significance \emph{is} the verdict} (a three-decision test). For a claim that names a direction: significant in that direction $\rightarrow$ supported; significant in the \emph{opposite} direction $\rightarrow$ refuted; not significant either way $\rightarrow$ insufficient. Refuted always means significant counter-evidence, never merely ``unproven.''
\item \textbf{Directional tests are one-sided at $\alpha=0.05$ per side}, in the observed direction---chosen to maximize usable cases (under a true null the chance of declaring \emph{some} direction is ${\approx}2\alpha$).
\item \textbf{No absolute-percentage claims.} ``In ${\sim}40\%$ of reports\ldots'' is a fact-check with no null, so those groups were retired.
\item \textbf{Two-cohort comparisons are directional only} (``higher after $v_1$ than $v_2$''). ``Do they differ / are they the same'' was removed: a test can reject the no-difference null but never confirm it, so those shapes have no clean supported/refuted.
\item \textbf{Report-level claims are exact facts} about one narrative---deterministic, no statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Whole-corpus results}
For every \textbf{case}---one distinct event-combination asked about in a cohort---we run its three-decision test over all 3{,}166 reports. A case is \textbf{decisive} when the test is significant: it can then be posed as a \emph{supported} claim (oriented with the effect) or a \emph{refuted} one (oriented against it). An \textbf{indecisive} case (not significant either way) can only be \emph{insufficient}. So the number that matters per template is \textbf{S = decisive cases}---the shared resource behind both supported and refuted. \textbf{Med.\ $n$} = median reports behind one case.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Headline:} the cohort-level groups supply \textbf{2,627 decisive cases}. Sampled to an equal 1/3 : 1/3 : 1/3 verdict mix (one claim per decisive case), that is a balanced benchmark of about \textbf{3,940} items---about 1,314 supported and 1,314 refuted from the decisive cases, plus an equal insufficient slice. Supply is dominated by the pairwise/co-occurs groups; sequence and multi groups are thin. New-group counts ($\star$) are candidate-inventory ceilings.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Reading the gradient.} Pairwise/co-occurs questions rest on ${\sim}30$ reports each, so most cases are decisive; sequence and two-cohort questions slice the corpus down to ${\sim}6$--$8$ reports, so most cases are indecisive---a property of the corpus, not the tests, and what makes the verdicts non-trivial.
\subsection*{Benchmark composition (target)}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Verdict mix:} ${\approx}1/3$ supported, $1/3$ refuted, $1/3$ insufficient per group---equal thirds, so every trivial policy (always-supported ``yes-man,'' always-insufficient) scores only ${\approx}33\%$.
\item \textbf{Sourcing:} supported and refuted are drawn from the decisive cases, one claim per case and from \emph{different} event combinations (never the A$\rightarrow$B / B$\rightarrow$A mirror of a single case). Budget per group ${\approx}1.5S$; groups with tiny $S$ (sequence) are taken in full and flagged as starved.
\item \textbf{Insufficient spans three kinds:} \emph{underpowered} (co-occur enough, no significant effect), \emph{sparse} (co-occur but below the sample-size floor), and \emph{not-locatable} (events absent or never co-occurring, $n=0$)---the last added deliberately, to test that the framework answers ``insufficient'' instead of inventing a relationship.
\item \textbf{Grading:} per-class precision/recall and macro-F1, not bare accuracy.
\end{itemize}

The roster went from 30 to \textbf{24} groups. Retired:

\toprule
\textbf{Groups} & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{Why removed} \\ \midrule
P-V2, C-V2, S-V2 (old) & PROP & absolute-rate fact-check -- no principled null (P-V2/C-V2 IDs reused below) \\
P-M1, C-M1, S-M1 & rate differ & ``differ'' has no clean refuted (can't accept H0) \\

F-M2 & differ-first & same defect as the other differ groups \\
\bottomrule\end{tabularx}
\medskip\noindent Added three single-cohort groups with principled nulls: \textbf{P-V2} (does $A$ precede $B$ more than $C$ does?), \textbf{P-V4} (does one pair's ordering beat another's?), \textbf{C-V2} (are $A$ and $B$ associated beyond chance?). The freed \textbf{P-V2}/\textbf{C-V2} IDs are reassigned to these new groups (the retired PROP claims no longer exist); \textbf{P-V4} is a new ID. Reworded \textbf{S-V1}/\textbf{F-V3} from vague ``typically'' to a testable ``most common'' (verified against the nearest rival).
\medskip\noindent\emph{Note.} New-group counts (P-V2, P-V4, C-V2) come from the candidate inventory (full corpus, most-frequent-events enumeration) and are ceilings pending pipeline integration; their \textbf{decidable rates} are the comparable figure. C-V2's association test spans the entire cohort (${\sim}1{,}000$ reports), hence its large median $n$.
