\section{Introduction}
This project builds a benchmark and framework for \emph{temporal hypothesis
verification over clinical narratives}. Given a natural-language hypothesis about the
temporal pattern of vaccine adverse events together with the relevant report narratives,
the framework returns a verdict---\emph{supported}, \emph{refuted}, or \emph{insufficient}---grounded
in the temporal structure of the reports. The substrate is \textbf{MedTempo}: 3{,}166
expert-annotated VAERS reports for three vaccines (Pfizer, Moderna, Johnson), encoding
ordinal relations (\emph{before}, \emph{after}, \emph{co-exist}) with no timestamps.
MedTempo is private scaffolding used only to compute deterministic gold labels; the
framework sees narrative text plus the hypothesis at inference, nothing else.

The positioning is \textbf{agentic temporal claim verification with statistical grounding}:
distinct from temporal knowledge-graph QA (which assumes a clean timestamped graph), from
scientific claim verification (which lacks population-level aggregation), and from classical
pharmacovigilance signal detection (which is not driven by natural-language hypotheses).
The work is currently in its dataset-design and template-generation phase; the results below
come from a full enumeration \emph{pilot} whose job is to characterise the corpus and
recalibrate sampling before scaling to the target benchmark of 10{,}000--15{,}000 instances.
\section{Current Findings (Pilot)}
The pilot enumerates every viable factual core in MedTempo across all 30 template groups and computes the deterministic gold
label for each; the full-enumeration run yields \textbf{289{,}216} candidate instances.
The central finding concerns statistical power under \emph{significance-as-verdict}: the
gold verdict is the significance-test result --- significant in the asserted direction
$\Rightarrow$ supported, the opposite $\Rightarrow$ refuted, otherwise insufficient.
\paragraph{Supported $\neq$ significant.} Over the \textbf{2{,}998}
cores that carry a definite verdict and an applicable categorical test (PROP and
report-level excluded), most are \emph{not} significant at $\alpha=0.05$. Running the
directional tests \textbf{one-sided} in the observed direction (2026-07-13) halves the
tail for effects already on the supported side and lowers the fail rate
(Table~\ref{tab:failrate}); the \texttt{multi\_differ} and GOF/homogeneity omnibus tests
stay two-sided.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Gold-vs-significance fail rate: definite-verdict categorical cores the test does
not corroborate at $\alpha=0.05$. One-sided directional tests lower it.}
\label{tab:failrate}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{Two-sided} & \textbf{One-sided} \\
\midrule
pairwise-precedes & 65.5\% & 61.2\% \\
co-occurs         & 73.9\% & 67.0\% \\
sequence          & 95.3\% & 95.2\% \\
first/last        & 70.2\% & 67.4\% \\
\midrule
\textbf{overall}  & \textbf{78.5\%} & \textbf{74.7\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
\paragraph{The ceiling is the corpus, not the budget.} Scarcity is in
the viable cores, not the reports (all 3{,}166 are used). Slicing the corpus into ``this
exact triple, this exact vaccine pair, strictly ordered'' leaves a median effective
sample of $n\approx30$ (pairwise), $\approx6$ (sequence), and $\approx7$--$8$ per cohort
(multi-vaccine) --- and that gradient is the significance gradient
(46\%$\to$8\%$\to$0\%). Four groups (S-M1, S-M2, S-M3, F-M3) have \textbf{zero}
significant cores. A larger per-group budget cannot help: it caps how many enumerated
cores are kept, not how many reports stand behind a question.
\paragraph{Design response.} (i) Significance-as-verdict makes the
supported/refuted verdict and the significance test coincide. (ii) Every claim must rest
on a principled chance null: S-V1/F-V3 are reworded from ``typically'' to ``most common''
(a modal-vs-runner-up test), and the PROP groups (P-V2, C-V2, S-V2) are removed --- an
absolute percentage has no non-arbitrary null. (iii) Cohorts stay small (median $n$
14--16; 70\% of single-proportion cores are low-confidence, CI width $>0.30$), so verdict
balance is enforced per group and the starved sequence/multi groups are addressed by
rank-based tests and category coarsening, not a larger budget.
\noindent\textbf{Status.} The benchmark is not scaled past the pilot
until the per-group supported/refuted/insufficient distribution is prototyped under the
final design (one-sided tests, reworded groups, PROP removed).
\section{Template Catalogue}
\subsection{How the templates are composed}
Templates are the single source of truth in three YAML files under
\texttt{data/templates/} (\texttt{pairwise.yaml}, \texttt{sequence.yaml},
\texttt{first\_last.yaml}); the advisor-facing \texttt{.docx} is a generated artefact.
Each \textbf{group} is a typed temporal question with a fixed slot schema, and is composed
along four orthogonal axes:
\begin{description}
  \item[Family / primitive] the temporal relation being tested:
  \texttt{precedes} or \texttt{co\_occurs} (pairwise, two events), \texttt{sequence}
  (strict ordering of three events), or \texttt{first\_last} (superlative argmax over a
  candidate set).
  \item[Level (cohort scope)] \texttt{report} (a single narrative), \texttt{vaccine}
  (aggregate over one vaccine's cohort), or \texttt{multi} (compare two vaccine cohorts).
  \item[Answer type] \texttt{BOOL} (yes/no), \texttt{CHOICE} (pick the correct option),
  or \texttt{PROP} (assert a proportion; supported if within 5 percentage points of truth).
  \item[Register (surface form)] each group is mechanically projected into four
  registers---\textbf{Original} (canonical seed), \textbf{Research}, \textbf{Media}, and
  \textbf{Clinical}---with no LLM rewriting. A negation modifier is additionally applied to
  a subset of BOOL instances at generation time (flipping both the assertion and the gold
  verdict); it is not stored in the YAML.
\end{description}
Every group instantiates both a \textbf{question} and a \textbf{claim} per register, so each group yields $4\times2$ surface forms before slot-filling and negation. The $30$ groups break down by family and level as follows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{Report} & \textbf{Vaccine} & \textbf{Multi} & \textbf{Total} \\
\midrule
\texttt{pairwise} & 3 & 6 & 4 & 13 \\
\texttt{sequence} & 1 & 4 & 3 & 8 \\
\texttt{first\_last} & 3 & 3 & 3 & 9 \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total} & \textbf{7} & \textbf{13} & \textbf{10} & \textbf{30} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\paragraph{Slots.} Placeholders are filled mechanically from MedTempo
term types plus a manual synonym table:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Slot} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{\{A\}}, \texttt{\{B\}}, \texttt{\{C\}} & event   & Event term type (+ synonyms) \\
\texttt{\{v\}}                                 & vaccine & Single vaccine \\
\texttt{\{v1\}}, \texttt{\{v2\}}               & vaccine & Two vaccines (multi-vaccine groups) \\
\texttt{\{r\}}                                 & context & Report identifier / contextual reference \\
\texttt{\{p\}}                                 & value   & Asserted proportion (PROP instances only) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\paragraph{Verdict mechanics.} \texttt{BOOL}: supported iff the assertion
matches the data. \texttt{CHOICE}: supported iff it names the correct option. \texttt{PROP}:
supported iff the asserted \texttt{\{p\}} is within 5 percentage points of the true
proportion. \texttt{Insufficient}: cohort below the $n=10$ threshold, indeterminable
ordering (ties, disconnected components), or required events missing from a cohort report.

\subsection*{Statistical methods (how each p-value is computed)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Binomial exact:} two-sided exact binomial -- sum the Binomial(n, p0) probabilities of outcomes no more likely than the observed k (null p0 = 1/2 pairwise/co-occurs, 1/6 sequence, 1/3 first-among-3)
\item \textbf{Fisher exact:} Fisher exact -- sum the hypergeometric probabilities of the 2x2 tables with the observed margins that are <= the observed table's probability
\item \textbf{Multinomial GOF (exact):} exact multinomial goodness-of-fit -- sum the multinomial probabilities of every table (same n) whose chi-square statistic vs uniform is >= the observed
\item \textbf{Multinomial GOF (chi-square):} chi-square goodness-of-fit vs uniform (large-n approximation, used only when every expected cell >= 5)
\item \textbf{Fisher-Freeman-Halton:} Fisher-Freeman-Halton -- sum the probabilities of every 2xk table with the observed margins that are <= the observed table's
\item \textbf{Homogeneity (chi-square):} chi-square test of homogeneity (used only when every expected cell >= 5)
\item \textbf{Wilcoxon signed-rank:} Wilcoxon signed-rank -- rank |d| where d=level(A)-level(B) (co-exist d=0 dropped), sum the signed ranks; exact sign-flip null for small n, else normal approximation with tie/continuity correction
\item \textbf{Mann-Whitney U:} Mann-Whitney U -- pool and rank the two cohorts' values, U from the rank-sum; normal approximation with tie correction
\item \textbf{Friedman:} Friedman -- rank the three events within each report (ties averaged), chi-square (df=2) on the rank sums
\item \textbf{Kendall's W:} Kendall's W -- normalised concordance (0..1) of the within-report rankings; p from chi-square on n(k-1)W
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Reading.} \textbf{R4} (temporal-gap magnitude) and \textbf{R3} (conditional antecedent) are the richest ($\geq37\%$ significant, hundreds--thousands of significant cores). \textbf{R5} (association vs independence) is the strongest \emph{co-occurs} replacement: each core rests on the whole cohort ($n\approx1{,}000$), so a significant result is robust even though only $25\%$ of top-event pairs are positively associated. \textbf{R1} is solid; \textbf{R2} is thin (few viable cores). \textbf{R6} is essentially a fixed S-V4 ($\approx29$ significant). \textbf{R7} (twice-chance) and \textbf{R8} (strongest-pair) are too hard / degenerate ($\leq5\%$; R8 yields \emph{zero}) and are not recommended. Suggested adoption: $\text{P-V2}\to\text{R3}$, $\text{C-V2}\to\text{R5}$, with \textbf{R4} and \textbf{R1} as additional well-powered groups; drop R7 and R8. (Counts are ceilings from a top-$K$ enumeration, so absolute \emph{Viable} values are not the final benchmark size.)
